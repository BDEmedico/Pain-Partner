# Referral letter

Use when Victoria asks for a referral letter and supplies a brief indication plus her last clinic note(s), sometimes with imaging. Synthesize multiple notes when provided. Follow Victoria's edits; do not restore material she trimmed. Repeated edits refine the pattern without automatically discarding unrelated guidance.

## Length and output — both formats

- Aim for roughly 150 words, usually 100–180. This is a guide, not a hard limit: do not pad a simple referral or cut essential information to meet it. Concision is the default for every referral.
- Minimize reading burden. Use short paragraphs wherever they improve readability, with no fixed paragraph maximum. Do not merge distinct points into dense paragraphs just to reduce their number.
- Include only details that explain the referral's purpose or help the recipient take over care. The attached notes carry the detailed history; additional words must earn their place.
- State the ask once at the beginning. Organize pertinent history, findings, treatment response, counselling, follow-up and correspondence pointers into short, readable paragraphs. Do not repeat the ask at the end.
- The attachment sentence may stand alone. Salutation and sign-off do not need to be compressed into the body.
- Deliver the entire letter in one plain-text code block, with no headings, bullets, bold, or placeholders inside it. Reissue the whole letter after revisions. Put only essential flags outside the block, in a few short lines.
- Before delivering, check overall length and reading burden; remove repetition, unnecessary background, exhaustive lists, and generic explanations.

## Clinical fidelity and voice

- Preserve Victoria's specific referral indication; do not substitute a different question inferred from the chart.
- Never invent findings, diagnoses, treatment responses, counselling, follow-up, or plans. Preserve the source's diagnostic uncertainty and attribute other clinicians' opinions.
- Omit nonessential missing details. If an essential ambiguity prevents a faithful letter, seek clarification rather than guess.
- Use full sentences and a soft, collegial physician-to-physician voice. Do not append specialty labels to colleagues' names.
- Report genuine treatment outcomes even when they complicate the working pain mechanism. Do not infer a nociplastic diagnosis from psychiatric history or negative investigations alone.
- Explain the patient-specific purpose of the referral when provided: for example, hoped-for pain reduction, mobility and quality-of-life gains from weight reduction. Keep anticipated benefits tentative rather than promising an outcome.
- Retain concise practical details that help transfer care, such as medication purchase/initiation, administration teaching, adverse-effect concerns and possible insurance coverage. Preserve uncertainty about access or coverage.
- Use generic medication names unless the brand or formulation matters to the recipient. Omit comorbidities that do not contribute to the referral question.

## Standard external referral

Use this format unless the recipient is Dr. Melissa Holowaty.

1. Open with “I would greatly appreciate your assessment/evaluation/opinion regarding…” and the specific ask. Add brief clinical context here if it saves space.
2. Give only the pertinent history, exam and investigation findings that support the referral question. Include relevant treatments tried or declined, why they were declined, and meaningful responses. These may share a paragraph.
3. Put treatment, practical barriers or documented follow-up into separate short paragraphs when this helps scanning. Describe clinic follow-up exactly as documented: a booked procedure, phone follow-up, or what will be reviewed. Use open-ended follow-up wording only when the source itself leaves follow-up open. Do not invent it.

End with “I have attached my latest clinic notes.” This may be a standalone line. Imaging and clinic notes accompany the referral; avoid duplicating their full contents.

## Dr. Melissa Holowaty — internal referral

Dr. Holowaty has direct chart access. These referrals often concern nociplastic pain, pain reprocessing therapy, or IV ketamine/lidocaine candidacy.

- Start with “Dear Dr. Holowaty,”. End with a sign-off such as “Sincerely,” or “Warmly,”, then a full blank line before “Victoria Tian”. No letterhead or attachment sentence.
- Paragraph 1 combines the source-supported working diagnosis and the specific clinical question(s). Prefer “nociplastic pain” when that is Victoria's stated mechanism. Preserve uncertainty when present. Victoria's supplied clinical shorthand, such as “chest wall pain ? 2/2 anxiety”, may be retained.
- Following short paragraphs give tightly selected referral-relevant context: duration/onset, established diagnoses, relevant psychiatric or substance-use history, and the pertinent investigation summary. Summarize negative workup rather than itemize tests. Include family history or the patient's stance toward a diagnosis only when clearly supported and useful to the ask.
- Keep a named colleague's established working diagnosis/differential with attribution and its defining findings. Cut unrelated structural findings, injury detail, and raw unattributed investigations by default; retain them when they materially affect the referral question.
- Include the patient-specific framework Victoria actually discussed, relevant treatments and outcomes, and current functional/self-management activities in short paragraphs as needed. State the role of interventions in enabling physiotherapy or active self-management when documented and relevant; do not add generic philosophy or undocumented counselling.
- Include relevant completed treatments and genuine outcomes. A booked procedure may be included as a fact. Omit forward-looking contingency plans and newly ordered investigations. A diagnostic block discussed as an option may be described as such, with the documented risk/acceptance framing; do not convert it into a definite plan.
- When Victoria supplies a consultant-note location, include the factual EMR/correspondence pointer directly in the letter: consultant name(s), correspondence source/date, page number(s), and any takeaway she explicitly provides. It may have its own short paragraph for readability. Do not invent the contents of unseen correspondence.
- Integrate any candidacy-relevant complicating factor into the existing paragraphs. Do not repeat the ask at the end.

## Approved tone: warm, appreciative and collegial

Use Victoria's preferred warmer physician-to-physician tone while keeping referrals concise and clinically specific. Express appreciation for the colleague's expertise and contribution to care without flattery, invented prior contributions or repeated requests.

Useful wording, adapted to the actual referral:
- `I would greatly appreciate your assessment of...`
- `I would value your expertise in clarifying the pain generator and guiding targeted rehabilitation or further treatment.`
- `Your assessment would be very helpful in distinguishing...`
- `Thank you for your help with her care. I have attached my latest clinic notes.`

These are tone examples, not sentences to include mechanically or all at once. State the clinical ask once near the opening; later appreciation may clarify the value of the assessment without repeating the request. A brief thank-you before the attachment sentence is welcome. Preserve diagnostic uncertainty, factual fidelity and the separate Dr. Holowaty format. Do not add length merely to sound warmer.
