```text
I had the pleasure of following up with this pleasant 66-year-old female with low back and left anterior thigh pain. She attended for a fluoroscopy-guided left L4 selective nerve root block.

MEDICAL HISTORY: Hypertension.

SURGICAL HISTORY: Appendectomy.

CURRENT MEDICATIONS: Amlodipine 5 mg daily.

ALLERGIES: NKDA. No contrast allergy.

SOCIAL HISTORY: Lives with her spouse. Retired. No tobacco use.

PREVIOUS PAIN MEDICATIONS: Gabapentin — dizziness.

PREVIOUS / CURRENT NON-PHARM: Physiotherapy and home exercises.

INVESTIGATIONS
Lumbar MRI: Left L4–5 foraminal narrowing.

PREVIOUS PAIN PROCEDURES
None before today.

PAIN HISTORY
Left anterior thigh pain worsens with prolonged standing. Home exercises help her back pain.

INTERIM HISTORY
Her symptoms are unchanged. She attended for the planned diagnostic block.

Previous EXAM
- Motor: L2–S1 5/5 bilaterally.
- Sensation: L2–S1 intact to light touch bilaterally.
- Reflexes: Patellar and Achilles 2+ bilaterally.


IMPRESSION
Possible left L4 radicular pain.

Procedure performed: Fluoro, LEFT L4 SNRB.
Performed by: Dr. Tian

Consent and Preparation: Informed written consent was obtained. The procedure, benefits and risks were discussed, including no relief, increased pain, bleeding, infection, nerve injury and adverse medication reactions. Vascular injection, dural puncture with headache, epidural hematoma, temporary leg weakness and rare serious neurological injury were discussed.

Preprocedural screen:
Active infection: No active infection and not currently on antibiotics.
Allergies: NKDA.
Contrast allergy: No.
Anticoagulation: No.
Antiplatelet, excluding ASA: No.

The surgical safety checklist, including briefing, time-out and debriefing, was completed.

Procedure note: The patient was positioned prone and the skin prepared aseptically. Local anesthetic was infiltrated with a 25-gauge 1.5-inch needle. A true AP view was obtained and the endplate squared. An ipsilateral oblique view profiled the approach beneath the left L4 pedicle. A curved 22-gauge 5-inch spinal needle was advanced through the left L4–5 foramen, with final position confirmed on AP and true lateral views. Contrast injected through microbore tubing under real-time fluoroscopy outlined the left L4 root without epidural, vascular or intrathecal spread. Bupivacaine was administered at this position. No corticosteroid was administered.

Injectate:
- Skin local anesthetic: 2 cc lidocaine 1%.
- Left L4 root: 1 cc bupivacaine 0.5%.
- Contrast: 1 cc.

Complications: None.

The patient was observed for 15 minutes and discharged home in stable condition.

PLAN
- Complete a pain diary, recording her usual left anterior thigh pain separately from back pain or injection-site soreness.
- Telephone follow-up in two weeks to review the response.
```
